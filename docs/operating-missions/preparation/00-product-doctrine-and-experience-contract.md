## 1. Product doctrine

**Build the autonomy. Sell the outcome. Keep the owner in control.**

Renvani is the broader field-service growth and operations platform. Operating Missions is its outcome-directed coordination layer, not the definition of the whole company. The default owner experience can be mission-first while office staff and technicians retain effective role-based FSM workflows, manual control and exception handling.

| Principle | Required product behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Owner selects the result | Start with the business profile and one of the six agreed outcomes. Prefill known facts and ask only for missing business decisions, targets and limits. Do not require the owner to design a workflow, write prompts, select agents or wire the flagship products together. |
| Renvani prescribes the method | Assess the actual constraint and propose a supported, bounded method with its rationale, prerequisites and next review. The product supplies reviewed defaults; unknown business facts and unapproved authority never default to permission. |
| One coordinated business | Growth creates eligible demand; Revenue handles estimates and economic evidence; Operations schedules, dispatches and fulfills work; Retention manages appropriate follow-up and obligations. Command Center presents their shared plan, exceptions and verified results. These are responsibilities, not separate customer records or disconnected automation chains. |
| Operations constrains growth | Demand generation must account for actual service coverage, qualified capacity, customer windows and material readiness. A campaign cannot promise unavailable service; a high-value opportunity cannot override a hard qualification or scheduling constraint. |
| Simple owner experience, capable staff experience | Owners manage policies, approvals, material changes and exceptions rather than routinely moving data between modules. Dispatchers and technicians can perform their authorized work directly. Advanced controls appear when the role or situation needs them, not as mandatory onboarding configuration. |
| Evidence before success | Leads, proposals, queued messages, bookings, dispatched visits, completed work, earned revenue and collected cash are separate facts. Headline outcomes lead the owner view; activities remain available for explanation and audit. A successful workflow is not automatically a successful business objective. |
| Continuing responsibility, bounded execution | Preserve the business objective and review its next constraint after each authorized cycle. Continue assessment or permitted work only within current authority, readiness and budgets. No perpetual model loop, automatic spend expansion or silent renewal of expired permission. |

Mission selection does not remove the core FSM: customer lookup, estimates, scheduling, dispatch, field work, invoices and existing payment workflows remain accessible to authorized roles. Normal human actions and mission actions use the same domain boundaries, current versions and audit history.

## 2. Connected customer and service journey

The journey is not just **lead → technician → payment**. It includes an estimate/assessment branch, operational planning and dispatch before every relevant visit, fulfillment, financial closeout and appropriate retention.

**Profile and outcome → diagnosis and approved demand generation → inquiry/work-order request → emailed estimate OR scheduled and dispatched on-site assessment → confirmed scope and customer acceptance → explicit work-order conversion → service scheduling and dispatch → field execution → verified completion and invoice → mobile payment/reconciliation → reviews, memberships and future service → outcome review and next bounded plan.**

| Stage | Responsibility and required handoff | Evidence and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Business profile and outcome | Owner sets the business facts, desired result and authority; Renvani prepares the method using existing profile data. | Selecting “Get more bookings” authorizes neither advertising spend nor customer contact. Missing facts are named, not invented. |
| 2. Diagnosis, awareness and acquisition | Growth proposes/executes supported demand-generation work under approved scope. Revenue and Operations supply economics, service coverage and fulfillment constraints. | A new business can use a supported external route without existing estimates. An unverified connector remains assessment-only. Existing approved work and limits remain authoritative. |
| 3. Inquiry or work-order request | Office/intake validates service need, location, contact and estimate path; preserve customer, property, opportunity and source relationships. | A work-order request is not yet an accepted service job. Ambiguous customer matches require review. One need does not become several opportunities merely because several missions assist. |
| 4A. Emailed estimate | Prepare and send the correct estimate version through the approved path, capture the customer's decision and maintain the next action. | Sending, viewing and accepting are different facts. Acceptance may permit existing domain conversion; it does not establish a technician dispatch, completed service or collected payment. |
| 4B. On-site estimate or assessment | Operations schedules the assessment appointment, assigns an eligible technician/crew, prepares the visit, dispatches it and tracks arrival. The technician inspects and confirms or revises the estimate. | An accepted assessment appointment authorizes that visit, not an unaccepted repair or installation. Scheduling/dispatch must therefore exist before estimate-to-work-order conversion on this branch. |
| 5. Scope confirmation and work-order conversion | At the job site, the technician confirms the applicable scope and presents any changes for customer acceptance. The accepted estimate is converted through the existing supported domain command. | Customer acceptance of the current version and actor authority are required. If remote acceptance already created the work order, retain and confirm that record; do not convert it again. Assessment-to-service links follow the accepted identity policy. |
| 6. Service scheduling and dispatch | Plan the accepted work using current capacity, qualifications, duration/travel, customer windows and required materials. Confirm the appointment and assigned technician/crew, then dispatch. | Same-visit service is allowed only when accepted scope, authority, resources and current feasibility all permit it. Otherwise schedule a later visit with the original lineage intact. Assignment or dispatch alone is not completion. |
| 7. Field execution | The technician uses the field app for next job, navigation, on-my-way/arrival/start status, work tracking, time, notes, photos, required forms/checklists, product lookup, actual material use and customer-approved scope changes. | Field evidence belongs to the canonical job and accepted scope. Poor connectivity exposes pending/failed synchronization; local taps or retries cannot manufacture server-confirmed completion or duplicate work. |
| 8. Operational closeout and invoicing | Verify required work evidence, customer sign-off where required, materials/time and unfinished items. Finalize the correct service allocations/invoice through existing services. | An incomplete task, blocked follow-up visit or missing required documentation remains visible. Completed but unbilled work is not silently lost or priced from an estimate fallback. |
| 9. Mobile payment and reconciliation | Present and collect payment through the existing authorized mobile/provider flow after job completion where that path is supported; provide the correct receipt and balance. | Payment requested, processing, failed/unknown and confirmed collection remain distinct. No blind repeat charge. Existing deposits/terms remain separate. New payment or charging capabilities still need separate authority. |
| 10. Retention and service recovery | Apply appropriate review requests, membership/agreement offers, renewal and due-service follow-up. Route complaints, callbacks and warranty/rework to operational resolution. | A review request is not a posted review, an offer is not enrollment, and agreement benefits are not automatically new paid sales. Respect current permission, customer acceptance, approved terms, billing and fulfillment obligations. Missing adapters become named blocked/manual steps, not silent success. |
| 11. Owner outcome review | Command Center shows the objective, verified progress, remaining bottleneck, critical operational exceptions, required decisions and next review. | Count each business result once. Separate earned revenue from collected cash and observed results from estimated causal effect. The next plan remains bounded by current policy and authority. |

The same customer can have several legitimate needs, appointments or visits. Preserve the relationship between them rather than forcing everything into one job or cloning disconnected jobs. Exact split/merge and assessment-to-service conversion semantics remain subject to OM-D07 and current domain contracts.

## 3. Operations is a full responsibility, not one workflow box

The following processes must be explicit in the selected method's plan and acceptance. They apply to assessment visits, accepted service work and relevant recurring/return visits. A mission does not need to run all of them on every cycle.

| Operational process | Required behavior | Exception and verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work queues and triage | Keep new requests, unassigned work, estimates awaiting a next action, scheduled work and incomplete jobs distinguishable. Use verified urgency, commitments and policy to prioritize. | An unassigned or overdue item has an accountable next action and reason. Critical committed-work risks cannot disappear behind a short list of routine recommendations. |
| Scheduling and capacity | Use business hours/time zone, technician/crew availability, qualifications, service duration, travel/buffers and customer windows. Use supported expiring holds and live confirmation. | Mission, dispatcher and self-service writes compete through the same atomic domain constraints. A provisional slot is not confirmed; stale availability requires revalidation. |
| Assignment and dispatch | Assign the appropriate technician/crew, expose accepted assignments and live job states, prepare the dispatch brief and provide navigation/job context. Track en route, arrival, in progress and completion separately. | Notifications need applicable consent/authority and actual delivery evidence. An assignment suggestion, dispatch request or sent notification does not prove the technician arrived. |
| Rescheduling and reassignment | Handle customer changes, technician absence, overruns and conflicts through current domain commands, preserving commitments and previous versions. | Revalidate skill, window, duration, materials and authority. Update affected staff/customer communications appropriately; do not send stale dispatch details after reassignment. |
| Job readiness | Provide accepted scope, customer/property history, access instructions, required forms and applicable service prerequisites to the correct role before the visit. | Unknown or changed required facts create verification tasks. Only qualified authoritative business sources can establish technical requirements; the model cannot invent them. |
| Inventory and material readiness | Check verified stock, location and existing reservations; prepare supported transfers, actual usage and replenishment recommendations for the specific job. | Unknown stock is not zero or available. Unreceived orders are not ready stock. Purchasing remains recommend-only unless a supported separately authorized purchasing capability exists. |
| Field work and scope changes | Support technician status, time, notes, photos, product lookup, checklists, actual material usage, estimate review and signatures/acceptance where required. | New scope/price requires the applicable accepted version and authority before work or billing. Duplicate/offline events reconcile to the same record with visible pending state. |
| Disruptions and unfinished work | Handle late arrival, no access/no-show, cancellation, missing parts, incomplete service, return visits and loss of connectivity as explicit exceptions. | Retain the reason, assigned person, next action, customer commitment and linked visit/job. A partial job is not quietly closed or counted as a second new sale. |
| Completion, quality and office handoff | Check completion evidence, actual time/materials, quality/service issues and invoice readiness. Escalate missing documentation or unresolved obligations to the appropriate office role. | A completion button or generated summary alone cannot satisfy missing required evidence. Billing, payment and fulfillment have distinct source states. |
| Continuing operational review | Revisit unscheduled work, unresolved estimates, incomplete jobs, invoice delays, capacity conflicts and due agreement obligations within the approved scope. | Resolve findings only against their specific condition. Assigning a next action can resolve a missing-next-action finding without claiming the customer accepted or paid. Persist unresolved work across reviews. |

Existing FSM services remain authoritative. Use the [source map](04-source-map.md), particularly booking/dispatch, estimates, jobs/field work, inventory, invoices/payments and agreements. This table does not assert that every listed process is currently implemented, does not approve missing service policy and does not direct Codex to build a parallel scheduler, inventory system or financial ledger.

## 4. Continuous management without unbounded execution

The long-lived business objective and an individual run are different concepts. A finite approved run can complete while appointments, collections or outcome maturation remain pending. Preserve its result, source versions, evidence and unresolved obligations.

On an applicable domain event or scheduled review, use deterministic services to refresh facts and identify the next constraint. Propose the next bounded plan, or continue a specifically delegated action only while its grant is valid and covers the exact action class, scope, resources and limits. Material changes to content, price, spend, recipients, service scope or commitments follow the existing fresh-review rules. Good results do not automatically widen authority.

Pause/cancel must stop new affected dispatch, including already-claimed but undispatched work. Preserve in-flight reconciliation and independently authorized shared work. Do not restart a cancelled run when a late receipt arrives. Waiting for a customer or owner consumes no continuously running model or optional execution environment. Model-budget exhaustion does not erase booked work, valid customer obligations or required payment/dispatch reconciliation.

For a capacity-constrained shop, the next constraint may be assignment or material readiness rather than more advertising. The owner should see the reason and an actionable next step, not need to discover which agent or module failed. The product supplies that coordination while preserving hard domain and financial constraints.
